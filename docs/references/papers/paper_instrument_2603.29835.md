## One-paragraph summary

This is the master instrument paper for SPHEREx (SpectroPhotometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and ices Explorer), an all-sky NIR spectrophotometric survey mission (0.75–5.0 μm, R≈35–130, 6.15″ pixels) built around an unobscured f/3 off-axis reflective triplet telescope feeding two cryogenic focal-plane assemblies (SWIR and MWIR) through a dichroic beamsplitter, each imaging through three linear variable filters (LVFs) that map focal-plane position directly to wavelength. The paper walks through the full verification chain — component-level screening of LVFs, H2RG detectors, and the dichroic; subsystem-level FPA, telescope, and electronics (ICE) testing; instrument-level cryogenic focus and spectral calibration in a dedicated KASI-built chamber; and in-orbit commissioning (IOC) — reporting quantitative performance at each stage against requirements tied to the mission's three science goals (fNL non-Gaussianity, EBL/galaxy evolution via intensity mapping, Galactic ice surveys). The instrument met essentially all requirements with substantial margin: wavefront error, effective PSF size, detector noise, dark current, thermal stability (<2.5 mK peak-to-peak on survey timescales, ~100× margin), spectral calibration accuracy (0.26% 1σ wavelength residual), data volume, and photometric sensitivity, with the sole exception of a narrow channel near 1.083 μm contaminated by metastable He airglow. It is a design/verification and commissioning report rather than a science paper, directly relevant to spectral extraction because it documents how wavelength is encoded by detector position (not a fixed dispersion), how the undersampled PSF is quantified via N_eff rather than FWHM, and several in-flight systematics (Earthshine, shuttle glow, lunar stray light, persistence-driving saturation, bright-source flagging behavior) relevant to any pipeline extracting galaxy spectra from SPHEREx exposures.

## Optical design and focal plane

- **Telescope**: f/3 off-axis free-form reflective triplet (three mirrors, unobscured aperture). Field of view (FOV) 11.3° × 3.5°. Circular virtual entrance pupil 0.2 m in diameter; the physical primary mirror is 0.4 m in its longest dimension to accommodate the pupil footprint moving across the FOV. The off-axis design eliminates azimuthally asymmetric diffraction spikes and allows internal baffle vanes to suppress off-axis stray light. A vaned fore-baffle extends over the primary, and a jettisonable dust cover protected the aperture through launch (ejected on command during IOC). Telescope built by BAE Systems, Boulder, CO.
- **Focal planes**: Two co-aligned focal plane assemblies (FPAs) view the sky simultaneously through a dichroic beamsplitter (DBS), which reflects λ<2.42 μm (to the SWIR FPA) and transmits λ>2.42 μm (to the MWIR FPA). DBS substrate is silicon (absorbs <1.1 μm, transmits well to 5 μm), tipped 22.5° from the chief ray in an f/3 converging beam, with a powered back surface for refractive correction and an AR coating on the back surface to suppress ghosting.
- **Pixel scale**: **6.15″/pixel** ("a low-resolution spectrum … towards every 6.′′15 pixel on the celestial sphere" — Introduction). Physical pixel pitch of the H2RG is **18 μm**.
- **LVFs and wavelength–position mapping**: Spectroscopy is produced entirely by linear variable filters (LVFs) — sapphire-substrate interference filters whose central bandpass wavelength varies with position along the filter. Critically: **"the position within the focal plane that a source lands on during an exposure determines the wavelength at which it is measured."** To build a full spectrum at a given sky position, that position must land on each of the six spectral channels across many different exposures/pointings (not within a single exposure). Each spectral channel occupies an approximately rectangular footprint of **3.5° × 0.2°** on the sky (i.e., the narrow dimension of the 11.3°×3.5° FOV divided among the LVF wavelength range).
- **Bands**: Six bands (1–6, increasing wavelength) span **0.75–5.0 μm** total. Bands 1–3 (SWIR FPA) and bands 4–6 (MWIR FPA), three LVFs integrated per FPA directly above the H2RG. Resolving power **R = 41.4 (bands 1–3, SWIR)**, and **R = 35, 110, 130 (bands 4, 5, 6, MWIR, respectively)**.
- **Wavelength progression functional form** (Eq. 1 in paper): λ_c,i(Ȳ) = λ_min·(λ_max/λ_min)^Ȳ, where Ȳ is the position along the filter normalized 0→1 between λ_min (edge, at the filter corners) and λ_max (at the filter's central X position); this exponential form keeps R = λ_c/Δλ (resolution element spacing) constant across the band. Because the LVFs were coated on a machine with rotational symmetry, iso-wavelength contours are circular-arc segments; the curvature ("smile") is quantified as the distance from the filter edge to the λ_min contour.

  Targets include a **quarter-channel overlap between adjacent bands**, built in specifically to guarantee continuous spectral coverage after accounting for coating/alignment error. Out-of-band blocking is defined as mean transmission outside −3Δλ<λ_c<3Δλ, required to be OD>4 (i.e., <10⁻⁴ leakage) across all bands.
- **Band 3/4 (DBS) transition**: The DBS transition wavelength (2.42 μm) was chosen to align with the SWIR detector's 2.6 μm cutoff and sit within the Band3/Band4 LVF overlap. Because the DBS reflectivity/transmissivity is rising/falling steeply there, the *effective* (as-measured) bandpass functions of bands 3 and 4 near this edge are squeezed (higher effective R) and shifted — blueward for band 3, redward for band 4 — relative to the nominal LVF-only design. This effect was anticipated and is baked into sensitivity estimates, but is a real spectral-response subtlety near 2.4 μm.
- **Detector readout geometry**: H2RG arrays are 2048×2048 pixels, read out in 32 electrical channels, 64 pixels wide each. The outer 4 rows and 4 columns of each array are non-photosensitive reference pixels used for reference subtraction.
- **Cooling**: Telescope housing, all optics, and the SWIR FPA must be <80 K; the MWIR FPA <55 K. Achieved via three V-groove radiators (passive) plus a dedicated radiator for the MWIR FPA, aided by three conical photon shields (LEO orbit) blocking Earth/Sun thermal input.

## Detectors (H2RG details, read noise, dark current, gain, well depth, nonlinearity, persistence, hot/bad pixels, reference pixels, readout mode and sampling, exposure times per band)

- **Device**: Teledyne HAWAII-2-RG (H2RG) HgCdTe arrays hybridized to a ROIC (read-out integrated circuit), 2048×2048 format.
- **Component-level acceptance criteria (Table 3)**, all met by 12 flight-candidate units (6 SWIR + 6 MWIR):
  - Cutoff wavelength λ_cut: SWIR (2.6 ± 0.1) μm; MWIR (5.3 ± 0.1) μm.
  - Quantum efficiency η_Det: **> 70%** over 0.8–2 μm (SWIR, measured at 4 discrete λ) and 0.8–4.4 μm (MWIR, measured at 5 discrete λ).
  - Read noise (correlated double sample, δQ_CDS): **≤ 18 e⁻ (SWIR)**, **≤ 15 e⁻ (MWIR)**.
  - Median dark current i_DC: **≤ 0.05 e⁻/s**.
  - Operability: **> 95%** of pixels meeting all criteria (i.e., up to ~5% of pixels can be non-compliant/"bad").
  - HgCdTe surface flatness: **< 25 μm peak-to-valley** (drives focus/PSF performance).
- **Well depth**: order **10⁵ e⁻** — as accumulated charge nears this, slope deviates from linear and eventually saturates; an "Overflow" flag (configurable threshold) is triggered on-board.
- **Readout scheme**: A custom "row-chopping" multiplexing pattern is used instead of conventional raster readout: after a row is read, the readout skips 32 rows before reading the next, returning to fill gaps later. This does not change the RMS noise of an image but aliases 1/f amplifier drift noise to higher spatial frequencies, away from the 5′–20′ angular scales targeted for EBL clustering measurements. Combined with interspersed reads of the reference rows and "phantom pixels" (Video-8 pre-amplifier reads with no H2RG input, used to subtract 1/f drift in post-processing), this reduced the 1/f contribution to the EBL signal band by **50%**. The measured correlated double-sample noise in phantom pixels is **< 3 e⁻**, versus **~10 e⁻** contributed by the H2RG/ROIC itself (added in quadrature with photon noise; the ICE/readout contribution is thus sub-dominant).
- **Sampling**: Raw detector samples are read every **1.5349 s**; a sample-up-the-ramp (SUR) algorithm computes an on-board slope estimate per pixel per exposure, reducing data volume by a factor of 118.1873/1.5349 = **77** relative to archiving raw reads.
- **Exposure time**: The nominal single exposure is **118 s** (precisely 118.1873 s) in the final flight configuration — increased from the pre-IOC baseline of **112 s** after IOC diagnostics showed spacecraft pointing settled faster than required, allowing the slew-settle time to be shortened and integration time lengthened for sensitivity. (Original 2018 proposal sensitivity projections assumed longer 134–146 s integrations; the shorter 118 s realized in flight was compensated by higher-than-expected LVF/detector efficiency and smaller effective PSF.)
- **SUR quality flags archived per pixel per exposure**:
  - *Transient*: sudden charge jump (e.g., cosmic ray, or bright moving satellite) detected mid-ramp; slope prior to the jump is registered; jump amplitude threshold is a commandable parameter.
  - *Overflow*: pixel exceeds a commandable charge threshold approaching the ~10⁵ e⁻ well depth; slope prior to overflow is registered.
  - *Early Transient*: a transient detected before a configurable time threshold early in the exposure. Governed by the **ZEROSLOPEON** parameter: if enabled, the pixel's returned slope is set to zero; if disabled, the pre-trigger slope estimate is returned instead.
  - *Early Overflow*: source so bright it overflows before enough samples exist for a slope estimate; flagged, slope returned as zero.
  - Exposure timing: pre-exposure reset occurs before slew-settle completion; the first two post-reset frames are discarded to let an electro-thermal transient decay before SUR accumulation begins.
- **ZEROSLOPEON systematic**: Pre-IOC default (enabled) caused loss of photometric information around bright stars, because charge blooming in the H2RG triggered early-transient flags broadly around bright sources; the field of zeroed pixels around bright stars was larger than expected. This was **disabled during IOC (practice survey 3)** and remains disabled for the operational survey — meaning flight data preserve pre-trigger slope estimates near bright/saturating sources rather than zeroing them. This is directly relevant to photometry near foreground stars superposed on target galaxies.
- **Gain/temperature sensitivity**: H2RG channel-output offsets have a temperature dependence measured at the mean level of **~150 e⁻/K**, with pixel-to-pixel and channel-to-channel variation; uncorrected drifts on orbital-to-survey timescales could imprint spurious large-scale clustering (fNL systematic) or diffuse power (EBL systematic).
- **Active thermal stabilization**: FPAs use a PID control loop (in the CEB) with Lakeshore Cernox CX-1080 temperature sensors and a Vishay VPR-220Z precision foil-resistor heater, with the control point on the detector side of the radiator-interface strap. Required drift limits (Table 6): **<0.67 μK/s slope over a 150 s exposure**; **<8 mK peak-to-peak over an 8-minute/30° orbital arc**; **<100 mK peak-to-peak over 6 months**; **<200 mK peak-to-peak over 1 year (double survey)**. All were met with orders-of-magnitude margin; in-flight the worst-case SWIR FPA peak-to-peak drift was **<2.5 mK** over survey timescales (~40–80× margin against the 100/200 mK limits).
- **Operating setpoints**: SWIR FPA 62 K, MWIR FPA 45 K — chosen conservatively pre-launch (colder temperatures proved achievable in orbit) and held fixed for the whole mission to preserve gain stability and match ground calibration temperatures.
- **Persistence**: Not quantitatively detailed in this paper (deferred to Fazar et al. 2025), but explicitly invoked operationally: IOC lunar stray-light observations excluded any exposure with the Moon center within 5° of the FOV "to avoid saturation resulting in persistent photocurrent contaminating measurements" — i.e., bright/saturating sources leave a persistence afterglow that contaminates subsequent exposures.
- **Non-linearity, crosstalk, dark current structure**: Measured at FPA level in test cryostats (Section 6.1.1) but specific numeric values for non-linearity and crosstalk are not given in this paper's text (deferred to companion papers); dark current structure is called out as a potential source of spurious EBL signal if not accurately removed, motivating the ≤0.05 e⁻/s dark-current requirement.

## PSF and image quality

- **Deliberately undersampled PSF**: SPHEREx pixels undersample the optical PSF by design, because in the photon-noise-dominated (ZL-limited) regime, point-source S/N is maximized by concentrating a source's flux onto as few pixels as possible — the opposite tradeoff from typical Nyquist-sampled imagers. Consequently, **FWHM and encircled-energy metrics are explicitly stated to be poorly defined/not used** at SPHEREx's spatial sampling.
- **N_eff metric**: Effective PSF size is instead characterized by N_eff = 1/Σp_i² where p_i is the fraction of total flux in pixel i. In the ZL-photon-noise-dominated limit, point-source sensitivity scales as ΔF_ν ∝ √N_eff (i.e., smaller N_eff directly improves sensitivity).
- **Pre-flight N_eff estimation**: Computed from an as-built optical ray-trace model (including diffraction) at 9 field positions per band (matching focus-test locations), with simulated PSFs at 1 μm sampling (18× oversampled relative to the 18 μm pixel), because N_eff depends strongly on the random sub-pixel alignment between source centroid and pixel grid (corner- vs. center-aligned sources give markedly different N_eff under sub-Nyquist sampling). Estimates averaged over 100 random alignments; focus uncertainty σ_P=±20 μm propagated via 3 PSFs per field point. IPC (correlates signal between neighboring H2RG pixels) is included and increases N_eff relative to an IPC-free calculation.
- **Compliance**: The telescope-only N_eff requirement is met at all sampled field positions/wavelengths **except the short-wavelength end of band 3**, at the extreme FOV corner, due to elevated WFE there (consistent with subsystem-level telescope measurements). Including the (better-than-required) spacecraft pointing jitter, the combined requirement is met with margin everywhere.
- **In-flight N_eff verification**: Measured directly from **1.1 million isolated bright stars** (photocurrent > 200 e⁻/s, no SUR flags triggered) using 7×7 pixel cutouts from IOC practice-survey data. Flight-measured N_eff values are consistent with the pre-flight optical-model predictions (Figure 13), and the total (optics + PSF) requirement is met with margin across the wavelength range, aided by pointing stability better than required.
- **Wavefront error (WFE)**: System (telescope-level) WFE requirement is **350 nm RMS across the FOV**, measured via 633 nm interferometry (ambient and cryogenic) plus a 3.39 μm interferometer check (ambient only, for MWIR focus). Met except in a small corner region at the short-wavelength end of band 3 (consistent with the N_eff shortfall noted above).
- **Ghost images**: 
  - *DBS ghost*: from secondary internal reflections in the tipped, converging-beam DBS; present for bright point sources viewed in DBS transmission (bands 4–6) and band 3 in reflection; severely attenuated for bands 1–2 because those rays traverse silicon, which absorbs at short wavelengths; because the DBS is tipped and the beam converging, the ghost is out of focus and spatially offset from the primary image. Quantified by the product of both-surface reflectivities <r_D1 r_D2>: requirement < 0.015%, **achieved 0.0062%** (bands 4–6).
  - *LVF/H2RG gap ghost*: reflections reverberating between the LVF underside and the detector's top surface produce extended power at large radii around bright sources; controlled by minimizing detector-facing LVF reflectivity r₂ (<2% bands 1–3, <5% bands 4–6) and keeping the physical LVF–H2RG gap small: **50–120 μm** (lower bound set to avoid mechanical damage during launch vibration).
  - No azimuthally asymmetric diffraction spikes, by virtue of the unobscured off-axis telescope design.
- **Off-axis/extended PSF and stray light**: Quantified in-flight via a dedicated "moonshine" IOC experiment (Section 9.5) rather than a lab measurement (deemed impractical pre-launch). Response is spatially asymmetric within the FOV (stronger toward one side), attributed to ray paths that directly strike the tertiary mirror in the off-axis geometry; the SPHEREx data explanatory supplement notes this can produce **rare spurious artifacts when a star of M_AB ≲ 3 lies near a target field**.
- **Distortion**: Not given a specific quantitative distortion coefficient in this paper's extracted text; optical performance is instead characterized entirely through WFE and N_eff/focus-tolerance budgets (±50 μm focus tolerance at each FOV location, set by the requirement to keep the core PSF within a single 18 μm pixel at the shortest wavelength).

## Spectral calibration

- **Two-tier calibration**: (1) Component-level LVF characterization at VIAVI (bandpass shape vs. position, via spectrophotometer + translation-stage scanning), verifying λ_min/λ_max/R/blocking against Table 2; and (2) Instrument-level, per-pixel spectral response measured in dark mode in the KASI chamber (TVAC-4).
- **Instrument-level ("TVAC-4") measurement**: Full assembled, cryogenic instrument illuminated uniformly via a cryogenic Winston-cone coupler fed by a tunable monochromatic source scanned across the entire SPHEREx band. This produced **a measured response function, with dynamic range >10⁴, for each of the 24 million pixels** in the SPHEREx focal planes — i.e., a full per-pixel bandpass/wavelength-solution calibration, not just a per-band nominal curve.
- **Wavelength-progression residuals**: Fitting a central wavelength to each pixel's measured bandpass and comparing to the design progression (Eq. 1) across all six bands gives a residual scatter of **1σ = ±0.26%** (Figure 15). The largest residuals cluster near the DBS transition wavelength (long end of band 3, short end of band 4), where the DBS's rapidly changing reflectivity/transmittance edge "squeezes" the effective LVF response (raising effective R and shifting the effective center wavelength — blueward in band 3, redward in band 4). This band-edge effect is a real, characterized, systematic and is explicitly accounted for in the sensitivity model, but should be kept in mind for any flux/wavelength interpretation right at ~2.4 μm.
- **In-band vs. out-of-band leakage**: Component-level requirement is out-of-band blocking OD > 4 (transmission < 10⁻⁴) outside ±3Δλ around each band's central wavelength range; this was verified compliant for all flight LVFs.
- **In-flight spectral cross-checks**: The 1.083 μm metastable-He airglow emission line (Earth thermosphere) appears in every exposure at amplitude exceeding the ZL background, providing a bright, spectrally narrow in-flight feature usable to validate the wavelength solution — but it also contaminates that specific spectral channel, making it the one part of the SPHEREx band where sensitivity falls short of pre-launch projections (Figure 25).
- **DBS thermal scaling**: Because ambient-temperature spectrophotometry of the DBS could not be performed at flight temperature (geometric test constraints), ambient measurements were scaled to operating-temperature performance using an empirically derived **spectral shift factor of 0.992** (obtained by monitoring the half-power reflection-wavelength shift vs. temperature on a witness sample).

- **Flat field / gain stability**: Not given as a standalone flat-field-error percentage; instead controlled indirectly via the extremely tight FPA thermal stabilization (gain sensitivity ~150 e⁻/K, held to <2.5 mK drift on survey timescales) to prevent gain-driven spurious large-scale structure.
- **In-orbit results**: Noise performance in flight is described as "indistinguishable from measurements in the laboratory," and the projected all-sky point-source sensitivity at the end of the (4-survey) baseline mission matches or exceeds the original 2018 proposal projections in every band except the He-contaminated 1.083 μm channel (Figure 25), despite the shorter realized exposure time (118 s vs. the 134–146 s originally assumed), because of higher-than-expected LVF/detector efficiency and a smaller effective PSF (N_eff).

## Stray light, thermal background, Earthshine, sunshine, zodiacal light

- **Zodiacal light (ZL)**: Dominates the photocurrent in every exposure; its Poisson photon noise is the limiting (dominant) noise term in the single-exposure noise budget (Figure 7, 118 s exposure), with detector read noise sub-dominant across the band. ZL intensity is modeled using the data-simulator model of Crill et al. (2025), and photon noise is computed via the SUR noise formalism of Garnett & Forrest (1993).
- **Earthshine**: Governed by local zenith angle (LZA, angle between the payload boresight normal and local zenith). Photon-shield geometry reflects specular Earth-limb rays to space, but scattered rays leak through at LZA>24°. A dedicated IOC experiment (fixed celestial target tracked while LZA is swept up to 55°) measured the diffuse Earthshine signal at **LZA = 42° (anti-RAM) to be 0.05 e⁻/s**, peaking in the short-wavelength bands and **~40× fainter than the ZL background** — judged acceptable. As a result, the survey avoidance criterion was **loosened from LZA<35° to LZA<42°**, preserving 100% survey completeness.
- **Shuttle glow**: A newly discovered (in-flight) contaminant: diffuse emission peaking near **3.0 μm and 4.5 μm**, attributed to fluorescence from atomic-oxygen interaction with baffle paint, strongest at low RAM angle (angle between velocity vector and boresight), where the telescope baffle directly contacts more atmospheric atomic oxygen. Measured amplitude vs. RAM angle (Figure 24) led to a new survey avoidance criterion of **RAM < 70°**. Even after this cut, some residual shuttle-glow contamination remains in survey data; it is recommended to identify/discard affected exposures using the redundancy of repeated (deep-field) observations.
- **Off-axis/lunar stray light**: Quantified via the dedicated "moonshine" IOC campaign (44 celestial pointings observed cyclically over 4 orbits/4 days as the Moon's angular separation varied), specifically avoiding any exposure within 5° of the Moon to prevent persistence contamination. The measured off-axis response (band 2 example, Figure 21) is spatially asymmetric across the FOV — attributed to direct ray paths striking the tertiary mirror — and, per the SPHEREx data explanatory supplement, can cause rare spurious artifacts in survey data when a bright star (M_AB ≲ 3) is near the field.
- **He 1.083 μm airglow**: Metastable helium emission from Earth's thermosphere appears in every exposure at amplitude exceeding ZL, degrading sensitivity specifically in the affected channel (band 2/3 boundary region).
- **Sunshine/Sun avoidance**: Not quantified with specific photocurrent numbers in this paper; managed via the photon-shield geometry and inertial/zenith pointing sequences during commissioning (Section 9.1); the instrument maintains solar illumination of solar panels while blocking direct solar and Earth thermal input from reaching the cold optics via the conical photon shields plus V-groove radiators.
- **Decontamination/water ice**: A cooldown strategy (200 K → 150 K controlled gradient, decontamination heaters keeping optics warmer than surrounding housing) limited total accumulated water-ice absorption path to <100 Å (<2.5% absorption requirement at 3.0 μm). In-flight, the median photocurrent spectrum across the sky showed **no detectable 3.0 μm feature**, confirming no ice contamination on the optics.

## Survey operations

- **Exposure/cadence**: Nominal exposure = 118 s (118.1873 s), raw sample cadence 1.5349 s. Pre-exposure reset occurs before slew-settle completes; 2 post-reset frames are discarded before SUR accumulation.
- **Slew/observing pattern**: Survey uses a "look-ahead" slew-group strategy (Bryan et al. 2025) where exposures earliest in a slew group have the lowest RAM angle (most susceptible to shuttle glow).
- **Band sampling per sky location**: Because wavelength is set by focal-plane position (not dispersed within one exposure), a full spectrum at a given sky position is built up over many separate pointings, each contributing one narrow spectral sample per band. The projected end-of-mission sensitivity plot (Figure 25) is computed assuming **4 observations per wavelength** over the full 4-survey baseline mission (i.e., roughly one all-sky "survey"/scan contributes per repeat).
- **Deep fields**: Two mosaicked deep fields near the ecliptic poles receive many more repeat visits and are used for the EBL intensity-mapping Fourier analysis on 5′–20′ angular scales; redundancy there also allows discarding exposures contaminated by shuttle glow/Earthshine.
- **Mission timeline**: Baseline survey duration 2 years, structured (per Table 6's "6-month" and "1-year (double survey)" thermal-stability windows) around roughly semi-annual all-sky scans; four such scans (surveys) compose the baseline mission referenced in Figure 25's sensitivity projection. First full-sky map completed by December 2025 (~7 months into the survey). >1.1×10⁶ exposures collected by time of writing (survey ongoing into its second pass).
- **IOC practice surveys**: Seven practice surveys of increasing duration (0.21 to 3.3 days; Table 8) progressively validated default configuration, on-board SUR/compression, ZEROSLOPEON behavior, LZA/RAM avoidance criteria, and final compression bin widths; surveys 6 and 7 used the final operational configuration and their data are included in the public released dataset.
- **Data volume management**: On-board SUR (factor-77 volume reduction vs. raw sampling) plus lossless Rice compression (bin width = σ_noise/2, i.e., quantization well below the noise floor) reduces data volume to meet the **260 Mb/exposure requirement** (averaged over 1000 consecutive exposures). Pre-launch simulation predicted mean 166 Mb/exposure (compression factor 2.37); actual flight data show mean **221 Mb/exposure, compression factor 2.86** — Galactic-plane/high-entropy fields compress less well but are sparsely distributed in the survey timeline.

## In-orbit commissioning results

- **Sensitivity**: Figure 25 (projected all-sky point-source sensitivity at end of the 4-survey baseline mission, from flight-derived median photocurrent, effective PSF N_eff, measured noise, and spectral response functions) shows performance matching or exceeding the original 2018 proposal projections in every band except the He-1.083 μm-contaminated channel, meeting requirements with margin. **No absolute 5σ point-source depth in physical flux units (μJy/mag) is tabulated in this instrument paper** — those numbers are deferred to Bock et al. (2026) and Ashby et al. (2026, in prep); this paper reports only the relative/graphical comparison to requirement.
- **Optical performance**: N_eff (effective PSF size) is stable and meets requirements with margin across all bands/positions in flight (Section 9.7, based on 1.1 million isolated bright stars), consistent with pre-flight optical-model predictions; pointing stability is better than required, so N_eff is dominated by the optics rather than jitter.
- **Thermal stability**: Both FPAs meet the 6-month (<100 mK) and 1-year (<200 mK) peak-to-peak drift requirements with **~2 orders of magnitude margin**; worst-case SWIR FPA drift measured **<2.5 mK** peak-to-peak over the monitored survey period (Figure 26).
- **Noise**: In-flight noise performance is "indistinguishable from" laboratory measurements.
- **Data volume**: Mean 221 Mb/exposure vs. 260 Mb requirement (compression factor 2.86); no data-storage/downlink issues reported.
- **Decontamination**: Verified successful — no 3.0 μm ice absorption signature detected in the median across-sky photocurrent spectrum.
- **Overall**: "There have been no indications of performance degradation, and the instrument is well poised to continue observations near peak performance throughout the baseline mission and years into any extension."

## Known instrument systematics and caveats relevant to data analysis

- **ZEROSLOPEON is disabled in flight** (changed during IOC): pixels flagged as "early transient" (e.g., from charge blooming near bright/saturating sources) retain their pre-trigger slope estimate rather than being zeroed. Pipeline authors should check flag semantics carefully near bright foreground stars superposed on target galaxies.
- **Cosmic-ray/transient flagging**: On-board SUR flags (Transient, Overflow, Early Transient, Early Overflow) are applied per-pixel per-exposure with commandable amplitude/time thresholds; "Transient" flags are triggered by cosmic rays but also by bright moving satellites — both are potential contaminants of individual exposures that must be identified/rejected in stacking.
- **Persistence and charge blooming**: A 5° avoidance radius around the Moon was imposed during IOC specifically to prevent saturation-driven persistent photocurrent from contaminating measurements, implying any very bright/saturating source can leave a multi-exposure persistence afterglow (quantitative characterization deferred to Fazar et al. 2025); charge blooming near bright sources also triggers early-transient flags over a broader pixel footprint than expected pre-flight.
- **Inter-pixel capacitance (IPC)**: Correlates signal between neighboring pixels; must be included in any PSF model (it increases N_eff relative to an IPC-free calculation) — relevant to PSF-fitting photometry.
- **DBS-transition spectral squeezing**: Effective bandpass functions near 2.4 μm (long end of band 3 / short end of band 4) are compressed/shifted relative to nominal LVF design due to the underlying DBS reflectivity/transmissivity edge; captured in the per-pixel response-function calibration but a real subtlety for flux/wavelength interpretation at the band-3/4 boundary.
- **Bad-pixel/operability**: Detector operability requirement is only **>95%**, i.e., up to ~5% of pixels may be non-compliant (dead/noisy/nonlinear) and must be flagged in survey-completeness/voxel-completeness terms.
- **He 1.083 μm airglow**: A bright, ubiquitous, spectrally narrow contaminant present in every single exposure, degrading sensitivity specifically in that channel below the pre-launch projection — the one band where the mission underperforms relative to original requirements.
- **Shuttle glow (baffle fluorescence)**: A diffuse emission feature (peaks near 3.0 and 4.5 μm) dependent on RAM angle; partially but not completely eliminated by the RAM<70° avoidance criterion adopted after IOC — residual contamination requires flagging via multi-epoch redundancy, especially problematic for diffuse/extended-source (EBL/galaxy) measurements since it overlaps ice-diagnostic wavelengths (3.0 μm water ice, 4.5 μm region near CO/CO₂).
- **Earthshine**: Small (0.05 e⁻/s at LZA=42°, ~40× fainter than ZL) but nonzero and short-wavelength-peaked additive diffuse background, present at higher amplitude for LZA>24° exposures.
- **Lunar/off-axis stray light**: Can produce rare spurious artifacts when a bright star (M_AB ≲ 3) lies near a target field, due to an FOV-asymmetric extended-PSF response tied to the off-axis telescope's tertiary-mirror ray paths.
- **Gain/thermal drift**: Although well controlled (<2.5 mK on survey timescales), the underlying gain sensitivity (~150 e⁻/K with pixel-to-pixel scatter) means any anomalous thermal event could, in principle, imprint spurious large-scale structure; the tight margin achieved in flight makes this a low-risk but explicitly-monitored systematic.
- **Ground-test coverage limitation**: Full observatory-level thermal-vacuum testing only reached 100 K (not flight operating temperature), so the highest-fidelity noise/dark validation at true operating conditions comes from instrument-level (KASI chamber) rather than full-observatory-level tests — a caveat on how directly certain lab noise numbers map onto true flight conditions (though flight data show noise indistinguishable from lab).
- **Compression is lossy relative to a fixed grid but sub-noise**: The Rice-compression bin width is set to half the expected noise σ (σ/2), so quantization error is small compared to intrinsic Poisson/read noise, but users combining many exposures should be aware pixel values are quantized at that level, not floating-point.

## Implications for extracting spectra of nearby galaxies

- **Wavelength is position-encoded, not exposure-encoded**: A single exposure gives only a narrow (R≈35–130) spectral slice at each sky position, set by where that position falls on the LVF; assembling a spectrum requires combining many separate pointings, each contributing one wavelength sample per band. Different parts of the same extended galaxy, imaged in one exposure, are sampled at *different* actual wavelengths within a band depending on focal-plane position — the per-pixel response-function calibration (24 million individually characterized bandpasses, dynamic range >10⁴) is essential input, not the nominal band-average bandpass.
- **Effective aperture / PSF for photometry**: Because the PSF is deliberately undersampled (no meaningful FWHM), point-source or semi-resolved aperture photometry must use the empirical N_eff formalism (N_eff = 1/Σp_i²) and account for sub-pixel-phase dependence (corner- vs. center-aligned sources give different effective PSF sizes). S/N scales as 1/√N_eff, with N_eff varying by band and by the pixel phase of each individual exposure/dither — an aperture correction derived from one band will not transfer directly to another.
- **6.15″ pixels set the native spatial sampling**, combined with the 3.5°×0.2° per-band spectral-channel footprint; extended-source extraction must treat each pixel as carrying its own per-pixel-calibrated bandpass and stitch these into spatial-spectral cubes only after accounting for band-specific N_eff and DBS band-edge squeezing near 2.4 μm.
- **Band-edge caution (~2.4 μm)**: the band 3/4 boundary has a measurably distorted (squeezed, shifted) effective bandpass; flux/line measurements straddling it should rely on the per-pixel calibrated response function rather than nominal Table-2 edges.
- **R and spectral sampling**: With R = 41 (bands 1–3) and R = 35–130 (bands 4–6), SPHEREx spectra are far too coarse to resolve individual emission/absorption lines typical of PSB diagnostics; line-flux/EW measurements should propagate the calibrated per-pixel bandpass (not a boxcar band average) to model line dilution.
- **Expected S/N regime**: ZL photon noise dominates the per-exposure noise budget at essentially all wavelengths; detector read noise (≤18 e⁻ SWIR, ≤15 e⁻ MWIR CDS, ICE contribution <3 e⁻) is sub-dominant. S/N per single exposure will be low for faint/unresolved galaxies (~4 revisits/band over the baseline mission for typical sky coverage, far higher in the two deep fields); most useful nearby-galaxy spectra require coadding many revisits.
- **Systematics to screen for**: shuttle glow (3.0/4.5 μm, RAM-angle dependent, only partially removed by RAM<70° — overlaps water-ice/CO/CO₂ diagnostics relevant to PSB dust/ISM work), Earthshine (LZA-dependent, short-wavelength-peaked), He 1.083 μm airglow (every exposure, degrades that channel specifically), persistence/charge blooming near bright foreground stars, and cosmic-ray/satellite transients flagged by SUR (check ZEROSLOPEON assumptions near bright sources).
- **No flux-calibration-uncertainty number is given here**: consult Ashby et al. (2026, in prep) and Bock et al. (2026) for the calibration-uncertainty budget to propagate into galaxy-spectrum error bars.

## Glossary

- **SPHEREx**: SpectroPhotometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and ices Explorer — the NASA MIDEX all-sky NIR spectral survey mission.
- **LVF**: Linear Variable Filter — sapphire interference filter whose central bandpass wavelength varies with position, giving SPHEREx its spectroscopic capability.
- **DBS**: Dichroic Beamsplitter — silicon optic splitting light at 2.42 μm between the SWIR and MWIR focal planes.
- **FPA**: Focal Plane Assembly — houses an H2RG detector plus three LVFs (SWIR or MWIR).
- **H2RG**: Teledyne HAWAII-2-RG, a 2048×2048 HgCdTe near-infrared detector hybridized to a ROIC.
- **ROIC**: Read-Out Integrated Circuit, the electronics layer hybridized to the H2RG's photosensitive HgCdTe layer.
- **SWIR / MWIR**: Short-Wave Infrared (bands 1–3, 0.75–2.42 μm reflected off DBS) / Mid-Wave Infrared (bands 4–6, 2.42–5.0 μm transmitted through DBS).
- **SUR**: Sample-Up-the-Ramp — on-board algorithm computing per-pixel slope (photocurrent) from a ramp of non-destructive reads, with associated quality flags (Transient, Overflow, Early Transient, Early Overflow).
- **ZEROSLOPEON**: Commandable on-board parameter controlling whether early-transient-flagged pixels are zeroed (on) or report their pre-trigger slope (off, the flight default).
- **ICE**: Instrument Control Electronics — comprising ROBs, CEB, and LVPS; handles readout, on-board processing, thermal control, and spacecraft communication.
- **ROB / CEB / LVPS**: Readout Board (one per band, clocks/biases/samples the detector) / Central Electronics Board (housekeeping, compression, thermal regulation, spacecraft comms) / Low Voltage Power Supply board.
- **δQ_CDS**: Correlated Double Sample noise — the standard read-noise metric for H2RG/ROIC combinations.
- **ZL**: Zodiacal Light — the dominant diffuse NIR foreground and dominant photon-noise source for SPHEREx.
- **EBL**: Extragalactic Background Light — the integrated emission from cosmic history, targeted via intensity mapping in two ecliptic deep fields.
- **PSS**: Point Source Sensitivity — SPHEREx's core sensitivity metric, determining redshift-extractable galaxy counts for fNL measurements.
- **N_eff**: Effective number of pixels containing a point source's flux (=1/Σp_i²), used in place of FWHM/encircled energy because the PSF is undersampled.
- **WFE**: Wavefront Error, the optical-quality metric (RMS nm) tracked through the telescope, DBS, and full system.
- **IPC**: Inter-Pixel Capacitance — electrical crosstalk between adjacent H2RG pixels that correlates their signals and must be included in PSF models.
- **PID**: Proportional-Integral-Derivative — the control-loop algorithm used for active FPA thermal stabilization.
- **LZA / RAM**: Local Zenith Angle (payload-normal-to-zenith angle, governs Earthshine) / RAM angle (boresight-to-velocity-vector angle, governs shuttle-glow/atomic-oxygen exposure) — both used as survey avoidance criteria.
- **TVAC**: Thermal Vacuum (test/chamber).
- **KASI**: Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute, which built the cryogenic test chamber used for SPHEREx focus and spectral calibration testing at Caltech.
- **IOC**: In-Orbit Commissioning — the March 12–May 1, 2025 period of instrument activation, decontamination/cooldown, first light, and parameter optimization.
- **ADCS**: Attitude Determination and Control System.
- **M_AB**: AB magnitude, used to characterize the brightness threshold (≲3) of nearby stars that can produce lunar/off-axis stray-light artifacts.
- **fNL**: Parameter quantifying primordial non-Gaussianity, one of SPHEREx's three core science drivers (target precision σ_fNL<0.5 after a 2-year survey).
