## One-paragraph summary

This paper is the reference description of the SPHEREx Science Team's **map-making ("mosaicking") layer**, which sits downstream of the Level-2 (L2) calibrated spectral images produced by the pipeline of Akeson et al. (2025) and upstream of science-ready spectral data cubes and full-sky maps. It is explicitly a methods/architecture paper with illustrative examples (Cat's Eye Nebula, Heart and Soul Nebula, Bubble Nebula, M33/Triangulum Galaxy, Helix Nebula, full sky), not a systematic survey of validated data products. The core message is that SPHEREx's linear variable filters (LVFs) entangle spatial and spectral information at the image level — each detector pixel sees a slightly different wavelength depending on its y-position — so the map maker must handle image-level pixel masking, an optional "contour filter" that removes smooth zodiacal-light/airglow backgrounds by subtracting the mean within thin wavelength bins that follow the LVF's spectral contours, reprojection (binning preferred over interpolation or drizzle-style "drop" reprojection) onto either tangent-plane or HEALPix grids, coaddition with hit-count and mean-wavelength tracking per map pixel, and several classes of foreground/systematics mitigation (image-level smoothing/point-source masking for full-sky work, mosaic-level "deprojection" of simulated zodiacal-light templates, and "spectral interpolation" across narrowband mosaics to remove LVF-induced discontinuities/"banding" near sharp emission lines). The whole design emphasizes strict **linearity**: every operation, when applied consistently, allows the total map to be decomposed as the sum of maps of individual physical/instrumental components (zodiacal light, stars, DGL, EBL, photon noise, read noise, dark current), which is validated with the SPHEREx Sky Simulator. Two self-consistency null tests (survey-1 vs. survey-2 map differences) and several qualitative/photometric comparisons (to WISE, to Planck/IRAS 100-µm dust, to HI4PI H I) are presented, but there is no single, universal validation metric — the authors repeatedly state that noise/uncertainty definitions and validation standards are application-dependent.

## Inputs (which Level 2 products, extensions, flags, variance, zodi model are used and how)

- **Primary input is the Level-2 (L2) "spectral image,"** as defined and produced by the SSDC pipeline of Akeson et al. (2025); publicly available via IRSA (https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/Missions/spherex.html). L2 images are the fundamental, unreprojected per-exposure unit the map maker operates on (Sec. 3.1).
- **Astrometric solution and quality flag:** each L2 image carries a fine-astrometry figure of merit; the map maker "typically restrict[s] to the images that achieve the highest astrometry rating (alignment < 1″)" (consistent with the `FINAST=0` flag described in the pipeline paper). Lower-rated images may be permitted for coarse diffuse-structure mapping where astrometric precision matters less.
- **Pixel flags:** L2 images provide per-pixel flags for detector pathologies (nonfunctional pixels, on-board-detected transients, irrecoverable saturation, strong image persistence, and similar issues). These flags feed the "flag mask" (Sec. 3.2), which is retained through filtering (unlike the source/object mask, which is temporary) and therefore permanently removes information from the map-making chain; the authors note it may sometimes be preferable to be more tolerant of certain flags for bright-source science.
- **Calibration already baked into L2:** dark current subtracted (estimated in-flight, since SPHEREx has no in-flight shutter), flat-fielded (relative gain), and absolute-gain calibrated to convert from photocurrent (e⁻ s⁻¹) to specific intensity in **MJy/sr**. The map maker does not redo these steps.
- **Per-pixel wavelength ("LVF") information:** central wavelengths per detector pixel come from the measured spectral response characterization of Hui et al. (2026); this is used both to define spectral-channel wavelength binning (Sec. 4.1) and to track/average the "detector wavelengths" contributing to each map pixel (a key output extension of coaddition, Sec. 4.3).
- **Variance:** the paper does *not* treat the L2 `VARIANCE` extension (documented in the companion pipeline paper) as a primary map-making input; instead it takes the position that a single pixel-level variance is not well-defined for most science cases (Sec. 4.7) and instead recommends empirical approaches — simulated noise ensembles via the Sky Simulator, survey-1/survey-2 difference maps, background scatter around target apertures, or binning by Galactic longitude for cross-correlation variance.
- **Zodiacal-light model:** the Sky Simulator's zodiacal-light model (Crill et al. 2025) is used in three ways: (1) to build/validate the image-level contour filter, (2) as a fittable amplitude-scaled template for mosaic-level "deprojection" of zodiacal light (Secs. 5.6, 6.2), and (3) as one of the linear components in the seven-component simulated-map decomposition (Sec. 4.6, Fig. 10). The model does not yet (as of writing) include atmospheric signals (airglow, shuttle glow, aurorae).
- **Source catalogs / simulated source masks:** simulated star and galaxy images (from catalog photometry, e.g., 2MASS, WISE, or purely synthetic EBL skies) are used, via the Sky Simulator, to build intensity-threshold-based masks for filtering and smoothing — not the real image data — in order to preserve linearity (masks must be independent of the data they are applied to; flag masks are the one exception, built from the real data).

## Map-making algorithm (projection, pixelization, spectral binning/channels, weighting, LVF handling, PSF, destriping, backgrounds, source masking, iterations)

### 1. Spectral/channel definitions (Sec. 4.1)
- Each of SPHEREx's 6 detector arrays ("bands") is nominally divided into **17 spectral channels** (matching the survey-strategy slew step). Six arrays × 17 channels = **102 nominal spectral channels** spanning 0.75–5.0 µm.
- Channels are defined by dividing each array's measured (not design) min/max wavelength range (Hui et al. 2026) into **logarithmically spaced bins**; edge channels are distorted by LVF curvature ("smile").
- Channel width is tunable: wider channels (e.g., 8/array) trade spectral purity for coverage; narrower channels (30+/array, up to 102/array for spectral interpolation, see below) trade coverage/noise for purity. Year-2 half-channel-width scan offsets support up to **34 channels/array without coverage loss** on the accumulated 2-year dataset.
- Two DBS-adjacent channels (last of array 3, first of array 4) are typically dropped due to gain uncertainty near the dichroic edge, reducing 102 to 100 (96 in the full-sky example, after also removing 4 helium-airglow channels).

### 2. Image-level pixel masking (Sec. 3.2)
Two mask types: (a) a **permanent flag mask** built from real-data pixel flags (transients, dead pixels, saturation, persistence), retained through the whole pipeline (removes real information); (b) a **temporary filtering mask**, built from *simulated* source images (Sky Simulator) with an intensity threshold, plus a circular object mask for extended targets smaller than the field of view (protects e.g. M101, M33, the Cat's Eye halo). Example thresholds (M101 field, b≈60°): 0.01 MJy/sr for m_AB>10 sources, 0.002 MJy/sr for brighter (m_AB≤10) sources — brighter sources masked more aggressively because real PSF wings are stronger than pre-launch optical models predict. Lifted after filtering, before reprojection. Optional sigma-clipping (median-filter-based, within narrow wavelength bins) can be layered on for outliers.

### 3. Contour filtering — the background/foreground mitigation step (Sec. 3.3)
- An *optional* image-level filter targeting **any spatially smooth component** (dominated by zodiacal light and upper-atmospheric airglow/aurorae/shuttle glow).
- Algorithm: divide each image into thin wavelength bins following the LVF's curved ("smile"-shaped) iso-wavelength contours (~1 pixel wide vertically) and **subtract the mean value within each bin independently** — a mean-subtraction ("contour filter"), not a fit to a physical template.
- Being a mean subtraction, it is exactly linear and can be applied to individual simulated components separately (Sec. 2.4).
- Strongly suppresses zodiacal light and helium airglow (1.083 µm) in example images (Fig. 4, Fig. 14); residual airglow striping can persist at reduced level.
- Tradeoff: removes target information on scales ≲ field of view (3.5°), so it is best avoided for targets larger than the field of view or for bright targets where zodi/airglow are subdominant (e.g., Orion Nebula in Murgia et al. 2026 is mapped unfiltered).
- A template-based filter (Sky Simulator zodi model, with a free amplitude) was considered but rejected as default since the model lacks atmospheric emission; authors "tend to prefer the contour filter, which is more aggressive... but also more robust."

### 4. Reprojection (Sec. 4.2)
- Mosaic grid options: **tangent-plane projection** (celestial or Galactic coordinates) for regional mosaics, or **HEALPix** for full-sky maps. Mosaic pixel size typically ≈ detector pixel size (6.15″), finer (e.g., 3″) in the deep fields.
- Two algorithms compared: (1) **binning (preferred default)** — intensity-weighted 2D histogram, each L2 image pixel contributes to exactly one mosaic pixel; faster, preserves more information, but can leave gaps when mosaic pixels are much smaller than detector pixels under shallow coverage; (2) **linear interpolation** — useful for contiguous coverage from sparse data but spreads/widens sources with every added exposure. Drizzle-style area-based reprojection ("Variable-Pixel Linear Reconstruction," Fruchter & Hook 2002) was tested and found relatively slow, with best information preservation only in the infinitesimal-drop limit, which reduces to plain binning — so it is not used as a distinct method.
- Quantitative PSF-widening comparison (deep-field simulated stars, using N_eff ≡ 1/Σp_i², following Korngut et al. 2026/Cutri et al. 2012): relative to the bare optical PSF, **binning widens the effective PSF by ~1.6× at 0.910 µm and ~1.3× at 4.694 µm; interpolation widens it by ~2.2× and ~1.6×**. Binning is preferred, especially in deep fields, since SPHEREx's 6.15″ pixels significantly undersample the optical PSF at short wavelengths (a deliberate design choice), and this undersampling — not the reprojection algorithm — dominates PSF widening at short λ.

### 5. Coaddition (Sec. 4.3)
- Images are coadded onto the mosaic grid incrementally; each mosaic pixel tracks (a) coadded intensity, (b) **hit count**, and (c) **mean central wavelength of contributing detector pixels**. This wavelength-tracking extension quantifies residual LVF-gradient/spectral-impurity per channel map: with dense repeated coverage (deep fields) the mean wavelength converges to the channel center, while under sparse coverage LVF gradients persist strongly (Heart and Soul Nebula example, 3.913 µm, 9″ pixels, 7°×4° mosaic).
- No inverse-variance weighting is described — coaddition is an unweighted mean per mosaic pixel, consistent with the paper's position (Sec. 4.7) that a single well-defined per-pixel variance is not established.

### 6. Self-consistency / null tests (Sec. 4.4)
- With SPHEREx revisiting many regions ("survey 1" vs. "survey 2"), a **map-difference null test** estimates total map-level variance (noise + systematics + unmodeled time-variable foregrounds), not just formal noise. Example (Bubble Nebula field, 4.176 µm): median intensity 0.431 (survey 1)/0.435 MJy/sr (survey 2) → 0.003 MJy/sr difference; median absolute deviation 0.077/0.078 → 0.036 MJy/sr difference; 93.4% of difference pixels within ±0.2 MJy/sr. Residuals include scan-pattern structure, artifacts attributed to zodi/airglow time-variability, and thin streaks from unflagged satellite trails.
- Caution: near sharp spectral features this null test conflates true temporal change with differing detector-pixel-wavelength distributions between surveys.

### 7. Deep-field-specific handling (Sec. 4.5)
- Two circular deep fields (north ecliptic pole; south field offset to −82° ecliptic latitude/44° ecliptic longitude to avoid the LMC) receive far denser, multi-orientation coverage, enabling finer mosaic pixels (typically 3″) and stronger LVF-gradient averaging. The Cat's Eye Nebula (NGC 6543) sits near the north deep field.

### 8. Simulated/linear-decomposition validation (Sec. 4.6)
- Seven components are decomposed and mapped independently for a Ursa Major field at 0.803 µm — **zodiacal light, stars, DGL, EBL, photon noise, read noise, dark current** — plus their sum, demonstrating linearity end-to-end through the real masking/filtering/reprojection/coaddition chain.

### 9. Full-sky mapping specifics (Sec. 6)
- A full detector-pixel grid would require ~1.4×10¹⁰ pixels; the paper prefers coarser HEALPix pixelizations. Detector-pixel-matched would be Nside=32768 (6.4″); worked full-sky examples instead use **Nside=512 (6.9′)** and **Nside=2048 (1.7′)**, comparable to Planck's typical Nside=2048/1.7′.
- **Pre-reprojection Gaussian smoothing (Sec. 6.4)** is applied as anti-aliasing before binning to the coarse grid — FWHM ≥ 2× the map pixel size (FWHM=20′ for Nside=512; FWHM=4′ example for Nside=2048). Distinct from image-level filtering; applicable to any target grid.
- **Point-source removal for diffuse-structure studies (Sec. 6.5):** an image-level source mask applied before smoothing suppresses (not eliminates) point sources while preserving diffuse/nebular structure (used by Murgia et al. 2026 for Galactic-plane PAH/Brackett-α maps). Full PSF-deprojection (fitting and subtracting sources rather than masking) is flagged as future work.
- **Deprojection of zodiacal light (Sec. 6.2):** jointly fits an amplitude-scaled zodi template plus a monopole offset (masking |b|<45° and bright sources during the fit), then subtracts only the best-fit zodi component. Demonstrated at 0.763 µm: the ecliptic-plane S-curve is substantially suppressed while large-scale Galactic features are preserved.
- **Full-sky cross-correlation example (Sec. 6.3):** Nside=512 maps correlated against two dust tracers — Planck/IRAS/DIRBE 100-µm thermal dust (Abergel et al. 2014) and HI4PI H I column density (Lenz et al. 2017, capped at line-of-sight velocity ≤90 km/s to reject extragalactic H I) — via a simultaneous per-channel fit of {zodi template, monopole, one dust tracer}. Sky masked to N_HI ≤10²¹ cm⁻² (71.8% of sky retained) plus 2σ bright-source clipping (dim stars remain and partially contaminate). Variance from 10 Galactic-longitude bins.

### 10. LVF-gradient entanglement and spectral interpolation (Sec. 5.5)
- Near sharp emission lines, the intra-channel LVF wavelength gradient produces unphysical spatial artifacts ("sawtooth"/"banding," shared terminology with Hora et al. 2026), since line strength depends sensitively on each detector pixel's exact wavelength.
- **Mitigation:** construct narrowband sub-channel mosaics (example: each nominal channel split into 6 parts → 102 sub-channels/array) and interpolate along each mosaic pixel (monotonic cubic spline, to avoid ringing) to a target wavelength (e.g., 4.6947 µm H₂ 0-0 S(9) for the Helix Nebula); typically ≤30 narrowband mosaics contribute, independent of area. Significantly suppresses but does not eliminate banding; residual scan-correlated structure can persist. This is a **model-independent** alternative to the image-level, profile-informed correction of Hora et al. (2026), useful when the true spectral profile is uncertain or a smaller product count is preferred.

### 11. Continuum subtraction (Sec. 5.3)
- Isolates broad features (e.g., 3.3 µm PAH) by fitting/subtracting a **local linear continuum** from two flanking nominal channels sufficiently separated from the feature (worked example: 3.124 and 3.603 µm bracketing 3.3 µm PAH in M33). The linear approximation leaves residuals, especially near bright stars; more degrees of freedom and finer spectral sampling would improve accuracy (cf. Hora et al. 2026's PAH-profile fitting).

### 12. Mosaic-level deprojection, generally (Sec. 5.6)
- Build a simulated template for any confounding component (starlight, DGL, zodi) and subtract a variance-minimizing scaled version from the mosaic; demonstrated for zodiacal light (Sec. 6.2) but stated as generally applicable.

## Output products (map formats, spectral channel definitions, units, resolution, coverage, availability)

- **Formats:** (1) tangent-plane-projected FITS mosaics for regional/targeted science (arbitrary celestial or Galactic coordinate tangent projections), and (2) full-sky **HEALPix** maps (Gorski et al. 2005) for wide-area/EBL-intensity-mapping and cross-correlation science.
- **Units:** MJy/sr (specific intensity/surface brightness), inherited directly from the L2 calibration.
- **Spectral channel definitions:** nominal = **102 channels** (17 per array × 6 arrays), logarithmically spaced within each array's measured wavelength range, 0.75–5.0 µm; alternate coarser (e.g. 8/array) or finer (30/array, or up to 102/array for spectral interpolation, or up to 34/array with 2-year survey offset scans) channel sets are possible and used for different applications in the paper.
- **Spatial resolution/pixelization:** detector-pixel-matched mosaics typically use 6.15″ (or 9″/3″ in specific worked examples: 9″ for the Heart-and-Soul coadd example to avoid binning gaps, 3″ in the deep fields). Full-sky HEALPix examples use Nside=512 (6.9′ pixels, with 20′ pre-smoothing) or Nside=2048 (1.7′ pixels, with 4′ pre-smoothing example). A detector-pixel-matched full-sky HEALPix scale would require Nside=32768 (6.4″ pixels).
- **Coverage:** full-sky survey achieves near-complete coverage roughly every ~6 months (twice per year per nominal channel); at the December 3, 2025 cutoff, coverage was essentially complete except for a few remaining gaps (closed by the time of writing) and residual Galactic-center dropouts due to fine-astrometry failures in crowded fields (to be fixed with improved astrometry in the Year-1 data release).
- **Explicit derived data-cube products demonstrated:** 100-channel Cat's Eye Nebula spectral cube (7′×7′, 3″ pixels), 96-channel full-sky data cube (Nside=512), spatially-resolved 3-aperture Cat's Eye spectra, continuum-subtracted M33 PAH map, deprojected full-sky zodi-subtracted map, dust-correlation spectrum.
- **Availability / release plan (explicitly stated):**
  - Public full-sky visualizations (through Dec 3, 2025 data), including PAH- and H II-isolating continuum-subtracted maps, already released by the SPHEREx Science Team (JPL PIA26600 press image).
  - "Specialized maps will be released to accompany future publications from the SPHEREx Science Team" (paper-specific, not a general public archive commitment).
  - **A public mosaic tool is planned for release by NASA/IPAC IRSA** (footnote referencing Akeson et al. 2025), incorporating the main map-making functionality described in this paper — this is the most relevant future access point for general-purpose galaxy-spectrum extraction, but no release date is given for the tool itself.
  - **All-sky data cubes are planned to be released 6 months after the conclusion of the first year of science observations** (per Akeson et al. 2025); the companion pipeline paper separately states fine-astrometry updates and the Year-1 release are expected around **November 2026**.
  - The map maker code itself is not released as a standalone public package in this paper (no GitHub/software citation for the map maker); only the underlying L2 images (already on IRSA) and, eventually, the IRSA mosaic tool and Science-Team-curated all-sky cubes.

## Validation and performance

- **Astrometric threshold used:** alignment < 1″ (images restricted to the best `FINAST` rating) for most examples; relaxed for coarse diffuse-structure work.
- **PSF widening by reprojection (Sec. 4.5, N_eff metric):** binning ~1.6× (0.910 µm) / ~1.3× (4.694 µm); interpolation ~2.2× / ~1.6×, relative to the bare optical PSF — i.e., reprojection-induced widening is wavelength-dependent and dominated at short wavelengths by detector-pixel undersampling rather than the reprojection algorithm itself.
- **Survey 1 vs. survey 2 null test (Bubble Nebula field, 4.176 µm, Sec. 4.4):** medians 0.431/0.435 MJy/sr → 0.003 MJy/sr in difference; MAD 0.077/0.078 → 0.036 MJy/sr; 93.4% of difference pixels within ±0.2 MJy/sr. Interpreted as evidence that large-scale features largely cancel, with residuals attributable to scan-pattern noise averaging, time-variable zodi/airglow, and satellite trails.
- **Deep-field coverage density:** e.g., ~280 detector pixels (6.15″) per HEALPix pixel at Nside=2048 (1.7′) in the smoothed full-sky/point-source-removal examples; hundreds of image pixels per mosaic pixel in the Heart-and-Soul-region HEALPix example.
- **Spectral calibration cross-check (cited, not reproduced numerically here):** Hui et al. (2026) validate SPHEREx spectral calibration using the strongest emission lines in the Cat's Eye Nebula core, using a spectrum built directly from L2 images (finer wavelength sampling than the mosaic-ensemble spectrum shown in this paper's Fig. 12, but methodologically comparable).
- **Photometric/morphological comparison to WISE (Sec. 5.3):** SPHEREx continuum-subtracted 3.3 µm PAH map of M33 compared morphologically (not pixel-by-pixel photometrically) to WISE W3 (12 µm) from the WISE Extended Source Catalog; morphologies broadly consistent, with a possible PAH deficit in dimmer regions at 3.3 µm relative to 12 µm. No quantitative correlation coefficient or flux-ratio calibration is given — this is a qualitative/morphological check only.
- **Dust cross-correlation spectrum (Sec. 6.3, Fig. 19):** SPHEREx maps correlated against 100-µm dust and HI4PI H I column density; results "visually consistent" between the two independent tracers; recovered spectrum shows steep short-to-long-wavelength decline (dust scattering efficiency), a strong 3.3 µm PAH feature, and modest Brackett-α (4.052 µm) and Pfund-β (4.654 µm) enhancements; small discontinuities at array boundaries (e.g., ~3.8 µm, array 4/5 boundary) attributed to possible unmitigated atmospheric contamination or coverage differences (this example is explicitly *unfiltered* at the image level — only lightly source-masked — so airglow/zodi residuals remain).
- **No single overall SNR, sensitivity, or photometric-accuracy figure is quoted for the map products as a whole** — this is a deliberate authorial choice (Sec. 4.7): "we cannot define a unique pixel-level variance, and we instead describe tools that may be helpful in estimating uncertainties in a variety of cases." Validation is presented per-application (null tests, external cross-checks, simulated-noise propagation, background-scatter apertures), not as a blanket map-quality metric.

## Known limitations and caveats stated by authors

- **No universal validation standard** (Introduction, Sec. 4.7): uncertainty/validation approach must be chosen per science case; none of the demonstrated approaches is "to be considered universal."
- **Filtering vs. target-signal loss is unavoidable:** the contour filter removes real information on scales ≲ field-of-view (3.5°) from *any* component, not just zodi/airglow; it cannot be used without destroying the target for objects larger than the field of view (e.g., the Heart and Soul Nebula), where filtering must be skipped or replaced by mosaic-level deprojection.
- **Background filtering can impart image-to-image offset biases** even for masked compact objects: "even if the object is masked, the background filtering may impart offsets that vary from image to image."
- **LVF-induced spatial/spectral entanglement ("banding"/"sawtooth" artifacts)** near sharp emission lines is not fully solved; spectral interpolation (Sec. 5.5) suppresses but does not eliminate it — "scan-correlated features may persist at a lower level" — and is explicitly contrasted with the also-imperfect profile-informed correction of Hora et al. (2026).
- **PSF and passband are not deconvolved** in the demonstrated spatially-resolved spectroscopy: "we have deconvolved neither the PSFs nor the passbands, so the relative line strengths may be misleading." Arrays 5–6 (R≈120) show less line dilution than arrays 1–4 (R≈40); PSF growth with wavelength weakens point sources and leaks contamination past source masks at longer wavelengths.
- **Sky Simulator fidelity gaps (as of writing):** simulated starlight is dimmer and simulated DGL brighter than real sky; the zodi model lacks airglow/shuttle-glow/aurora components — limiting how literally simulation-based masks/templates/noise estimates can be trusted.
- **Astrometric gaps near the Galactic center:** fine-astrometry fails more often in crowded fields; affected images are excluded until the Year-1 astrometry update (~Nov 2026).
- **Coarse full-sky renormalization can hide small-scale coverage gaps** (Sec. 2.2): the hits-per-detector-pixel convention averages over ~3.4′ pixels and can obscure gaps visible only on finer, regional maps.
- **Point-source masking suppresses but does not eliminate contamination** (Sec. 6.5); true PSF-based source deprojection, and image-level (pre-reprojection) point-source deprojection, are both deferred to future work.
- **Continuum-subtraction residuals** from the linear-continuum approximation persist, especially near bright stars; a higher-order or physically-motivated model is flagged as future work.
- **Weighting/variance is unresolved as a general framework:** no inverse-variance/noise-weighted coaddition is described; pixel-level variance is treated as not well-defined in general, dominated by non-Gaussian, non-stationary effects (unflagged transients, sparse spectral sampling, time-variable zodi/airglow) rather than simple photon+read noise.
- **Dataset is only ~1/8 of the planned mission** (Dec 3 2025 cutoff, ~7 months of 2 years, one full-sky pass); sensitivity, spectral homogenization, and artifact suppression are expected to improve substantially with subsequent passes, especially away from the deep fields.

## Relevance to extracting galaxy spectra (synthesis)

**When a map-based spectrum is preferable to per-exposure extraction.** The map maker is designed for two regimes relevant to nearby PSB galaxies: (1) **resolved/semi-resolved sources**, where Level-3 point-source forced photometry (Tractor-based PSF fits at catalog positions, per the companion pipeline paper) is the wrong tool because it assumes point sources, not extended surface brightness; and (2) **spatially-resolved, aperture-based spectroscopy across a galaxy**, exactly as demonstrated for the Cat's Eye Nebula (core/inner-halo/outer-halo apertures extracted from a 100-channel mosaic ensemble, Sec. 5.2). For resolved/semi-resolved PSB targets, the natural analog is to build a ~100-channel spectral data cube (Fig. 11-style) and extract per-channel aperture or radial-profile photometry (Fig. 12-style). For fully unresolved dwarfs/satellites, L3 catalog spectra are likely more direct; map-based extraction there mainly adds value for **background characterization** (the paper's own recommended method: scatter measured at random nearby apertures, Sec. 4.7) and for recovering sources that fail L3 matching/deblending.

**What map-based PSB spectra will look like.** (a) Spectral resolution is LVF-limited — R≈40 for arrays 1–4 (0.75–2.44 µm), R≈120 for arrays 5–6 (3.79–5.02 µm) — so narrower features are diluted/blended (explicitly flagged for line-strength interpretation, Sec. 5.2); (b) reprojection broadens the effective PSF by ~1.3–1.6× (binning; worse for interpolation and at shorter wavelengths) relative to the bare optical PSF; (c) each "channel" map carries a per-pixel mean detector wavelength that can differ from the nominal channel center, especially outside deep fields or near sharp features — directly relevant to near-IR H I (Paschen/Brackett/Pfund), He I 1.083 µm, and 3.3 µm PAH diagnostics that would show the same LVF systematics in a PSB host (optical Balmer/[O III] lines are outside SPHEREx's 0.75–5.0 µm range); (d) targets smaller than the 3.5° field of view are the preferred regime, since image-level masking + contour filtering can remove zodi/airglow cleanly without touching the target; (e) targets or extended halos approaching/exceeding 3.5° (relevant mainly for group/cluster-scale diffuse light) require skipping filtering or using mosaic-level deprojection instead, at the cost of retained, independently-characterized zodi/airglow contamination.

**Backgrounds and residuals.** Two mitigation philosophies are demonstrated, both imperfect: (1) **image-level contour filtering** (mean-subtraction per LVF wavelength bin) is aggressive and removes most zodi/airglow but causes target-scale information loss and can leave elevated residual *noise* near strong airglow lines (shown at 1.086 µm near He I, Sec. 5.1); (2) **mosaic-level deprojection** of a simulated zodi template (free amplitude + monopole) preserves target information better but depends on Sky Simulator fidelity and does not address atmospheric emission at all (no airglow/aurora/shuttle-glow component as of writing). Practical implication: always report whether image-level filtering was applied, and if so quantify target-flux suppression via simulation propagation or transfer-function characterization (Sec. 5.4) rather than assuming it is negligible. Unfiltered/lightly-masked analyses can show real spectral artifacts at array boundaries (paper's own example: a ~3.8 µm discontinuity at the array 4/5 boundary, attributed to possible unmitigated atmospheric contamination) — a transferable caution for any galaxy spectrum spanning multiple arrays.

**Stacking: exposure-level vs. map-level.** The paper does not discuss galaxy stacking explicitly, but its linearity principle (Sec. 2.4, Eq. 1: T(Σxᵢ)=ΣT(xᵢ)) supports **map-level stacking being equivalent to exposure-level stacking**, provided masking is derived independently of the data (from simulation) and no nonlinear step (data-driven sigma-clipping, the monotonic-spline interpolation step, per-object background thresholds) is applied inconsistently across stack members — this is exactly what the paper verifies end-to-end via the seven-component decomposition (Sec. 4.6). Practically: it should be safe to coadd per-galaxy cutout mosaics/spectra rather than returning to raw L2 exposures for every stack, but (i) hit counts, mean wavelength, and PSF width vary per object with deep-field proximity and LVF position, so these should be tracked per object rather than assumed homogeneous; (ii) contour filtering with per-object mask geometry is not strictly linear across a heterogeneous stack, so mosaic-level deprojection (a fixed, data-independent template) is preferable when stacking many faint galaxies.

**Concrete steps to reuse:**
1. Restrict to alignment<1″ L2 images by default; relax only for coarse/diffuse work, with documentation.
2. Build source/background masks from Sky-Simulator images and catalog SEDs (2MASS/WISE), never from the real target data, to preserve linearity.
3. Decide filter-vs-no-filter per target using the paper's own criterion (size vs. 3.5°, brightness vs. zodi/airglow, Sec. 5.4); for arcmin-scale PSB galaxies, contour filtering with a protective object mask (as for M101/M33/Cat's Eye) is the template.
4. Build a ~100-channel spectral data cube and extract per-aperture/radial-profile photometry per channel, background-subtracted using random nearby apertures with their scatter as the uncertainty (Sec. 5.2) — the most directly transferable recipe in the paper.
5. For strong near-IR lines/PAH features, use narrowband spectral interpolation (~6 sub-channels/nominal channel, monotonic cubic spline, Sec. 5.5) to suppress LVF banding, especially outside deep fields.
6. For continuum-normalized feature maps, reuse the two-flanking-channel linear continuum-subtraction recipe (Sec. 5.3) as a first pass, noting residuals near bright sources.
7. Do not rely on a single per-pixel variance; propagate Sky Simulator noise for a lower bound and validate with multi-survey map-difference null tests for a realistic total error budget, as done for the Bubble Nebula field.

## Glossary

- **LVF (Linear Variable Filter):** Physical bandpass filter placed above each SPHEREx detector array; transmitted wavelength varies continuously with detector y-position, so each pixel has a unique central wavelength. Source of the spatial/spectral entanglement addressed throughout the paper.
- **L2 (Level-2) image / "spectral image":** Astrometrically and photometrically calibrated per-exposure image in MJy/sr, produced by the SSDC pipeline (Akeson et al. 2025); the fundamental input to this paper's map maker.
- **Contour filter:** Image-level mean-subtraction filter applied within thin wavelength bins that follow LVF iso-wavelength contours; removes spatially smooth backgrounds (zodi, airglow).
- **Deprojection:** Mosaic-level fitting and removal of a foreground/background template (e.g., simulated zodiacal light) via a variance-minimizing scaled subtraction, optionally with a free monopole offset.
- **Spectral interpolation:** Constructing narrowband (sub-nominal-channel) mosaics and interpolating (monotonic cubic spline) along wavelength at each mosaic pixel to estimate intensity at a single target wavelength, suppressing LVF-gradient artifacts ("banding"/"sawtooth" patterns).
- **Banding / sawtooth artifacts:** Unphysical spatial discontinuities in a nominal-channel map near sharp spectral features, caused by heterogeneous detector-pixel wavelength coverage across the mosaic (LVF gradient not fully averaged down).
- **Hits map:** Per-mosaic-pixel count of contributing detector-pixel measurements; tracked alongside intensity and mean wavelength during coaddition.
- **Deep fields:** Two circular sky regions (centered near the north and south ecliptic poles, south field offset to avoid the LMC) that receive disproportionately dense, multi-orientation SPHEREx coverage every orbit, enabling finer mosaic pixels and stronger LVF-gradient/noise averaging.
- **Helium airglow:** Upper-atmospheric (Earth, ~650 km altitude) He I emission at 1.083 µm, the dominant signal at that wavelength in SPHEREx data; a major foreground for astrophysical He I science and a testbed for filtering methods.
- **Sky Simulator:** SPHEREx Science Team's forward-modeling tool (Crill et al. 2025) producing mock images with zodiacal light, starlight, DGL, EBL, photon noise, read noise, and dark current; used throughout for mask construction, filter validation, deprojection templates, and linearity testing.
- **Linearity (design principle):** Every map-making operation (masking, filtering, reprojection, coaddition) is constructed so total data = sum of per-component data propagates identically through the pipeline, enabling component-by-component (e.g., galaxy-vs-foreground) analysis and validation.
- **N_eff (effective number of noise pixels):** PSF-width/resolution metric, N_eff ≡ 1/Σpᵢ² where pᵢ is the fraction of a point source's flux in mosaic pixel i; used to quantify reprojection-induced PSF broadening.
- **DBS (Dichroic Beam Splitter):** Optical element splitting SPHEREx light between the short-wavelength focal plane (arrays 1–3, 0.75–2.44 µm) and long-wavelength focal plane (arrays 4–6, 2.38–5.02 µm); channels adjacent to the DBS split are typically excluded from science maps due to gain uncertainty.
- **EBL / IGL / IHL:** Extragalactic background light (integrated emission from all extragalactic sources, SPHEREx's primary intensity-mapping science target); integrated galactic light (the cataloged-galaxy-dominated component of EBL); intrahalo light (diffuse light from stripped/satellite stars, included in synthetic EBL sky models).
- **DGL (Diffuse Galactic Light):** Starlight scattered by interstellar dust grains; significant on scales ≥ the SPHEREx field of view; includes the Sky Simulator's 3.3 µm PAH emission component.
